# Clarify: what it produces, and how

**Discussion material · unbranded · for CFIT to use, amend or badge as its own**

*Working draft. Prepared for discussion with CFIT ahead of Joint Sprint Zero, 13 August 2026.*

---

## The question Clarify has to answer

Clarify closes on 22 October and releases the **Develop-phase programme**.

Three things follow from that, and they are worth deciding deliberately:

- What, precisely, is that programme a programme *of*?
- What has to be true by 22 October for it to be releasable?
- What makes its output referable in secondary legislation, rather than merely published?

This is a proposed answer. If there is a better one, it should win.

---

## The proposal in one line

> **Clarify agrees the requirements and the decision programme. Develop takes the decisions. Implement writes and tests the framework.**

| Phase | Produces |
|---|---|
| **Clarify** — to 22 Oct | The requirements the framework must satisfy, and the map of every decision that follows from them |
| **Develop** | Those decisions taken, each on a record |
| **Implement** | Normative text, conformance suite, interoperability results |

The Develop-phase programme that Clarify is asked to release **is** the decision schedule. Naming it that way gives Clarify a definite, checkable deliverable.

---

## Why requirements first

Every provision in the final framework should be traceable to a requirement the coalition agreed, through a decision that recorded its options.

That is what lets a civil servant ask *"show me why this clause says what it says"* — and get an answer.

Requirements must be **testable**. For each one it must be possible to say of a design "this meets it" or "this does not", without appeal to taste. Vague requirements are worse than none: they can be cited in support of anything.

Roughly 15 of them. Drawn from open-data principles, open ecosystem, and reusable trust.

---

## Why the decision map matters

Publishing the full map of what will be decided, before deciding any of it, does three things:

- **Nothing arrives by surprise.** The fastest way to lose a coalition's trust is a decision appearing that nobody knew was coming.
- **It sizes the work honestly.** The map is how Develop's duration gets decided on evidence rather than optimism.
- **It shows where the real disagreements are** — and, more often, where there are none.

Around nine strands. One root question each, decided together. Then four to eight decisions within each strand.

---

## What a decision looks like

Each decision produces one record:

| | |
|---|---|
| **Question** | One sentence, neutrally framed |
| **Requirements** | Which agreed requirements it is tested against |
| **Options** | Each with evidence, cost of adoption, cost of being wrong |
| **Resolution** | The choice, argued in terms of the requirements |
| **Objections** | Recorded permanently and named, whether or not they changed the outcome |
| **Consequences** | What this forecloses; what reversing it would cost |

The record *is* the deliverable. A framework arrives with its reasoning attached, or it arrives as an assertion.

---

## How decisions close

Every decision has a published closing date and one of four recorded dispositions:

1. **Resolved** — rough consensus reached
2. **Provisional** — a clear leading option, objections recorded, revisited on evidence
3. **Referred to CFIT** — on a full record, per the Terms of Reference
4. **Deferred** — with a named owner and a statement of what evidence would settle it

**The method recommends; CFIT decides.** This does not displace CFIT's final decision where consensus cannot be reached. It ensures that when CFIT exercises it, it does so on a documented record.

An undocumented decision is defensible by reference to who made it. A decision arriving with its reasoning is defensible on its merits.

---

## Evidence, agreed in advance

Tiers published **before anyone knows who can satisfy them**. Two axes, because they answer different questions:

| | |
|---|---|
| **Interop** | Does this work across independently built systems? |
| **Operational** | Does this work in real transactions, at scale, with real users? |

Plus alignment with adopted external standards, quantified analysis, and reasoned argument.

**The qualification that makes it work:** evidence generated to confirm a position is a demonstration. Evidence that could have refuted it and did not is evidence. Where a decision is contested, test the options you do not favour, and publish results against your own interest.

---

## Deciding, building, deciding again

Some questions are only answerable by building. The programme should run as a spiral, not a waterfall.

```
   DECIDE ──► PRE-REGISTER ──► BUILD ──► PUBLISH ──┐
      ▲          criteria       round     findings │
      └──────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

Before a build round runs, publish the options, the tests, and **what result would count as a failure**. Agreeing in advance what a bad result looks like is what makes the finding credible whichever way it goes.

**The pilot is a build round.** Product & Service Design and Trust, Legal & Policy are not competing for the same questions — they are two halves of one loop. The useful question is the cadence between them and how findings are routed.

---

## Participation without engineers

Build rounds must not become the mechanism by which the organisations with engineering teams decide the framework.

The influential step is **specifying what a round must demonstrate** — and that needs domain knowledge, not engineers. Conveyancers, agents and lenders know the failure modes that matter.

Three routes in: write the test cases, contribute real-world data, set the acceptance criteria.

## What this gives CFIT

| | |
|---|---|
| **A definite Clarify deliverable** | The decision schedule, rather than an undefined "programme" |
| **A basis for the Develop duration** | Sized from the map, decided on evidence |
| **Referability** | Every provision traceable to a recorded decision — what secondary legislation needs |
| **Competition-law comfort** | Open participation, published objective criteria, non-discriminatory access, no exclusivity: what authorities look for in standard-setting |
| **Partner confidence** | No participant's prior work is a default; positions are attributed and can lose |
| **A defensible final call** | Where CFIT decides, it decides on a record |

## What we would ask

1. **Adopt this, amend it, or better it** — as CFIT's own programme design, presented as such at Sprint Zero. We are not seeking attribution.

2. **Settle one question early:** is the Digital Property ID a *specification* anyone can implement, or an *instance* somebody operates? Both readings are supported by the workstream papers, and ownership, funding, liability and whether the pilot evidence generalises all turn on it.

3. **Move four architectural questions from Product & Service Design to Trust, Legal & Policy** — see the next slide. Their answers bind every participant, not just the pilot, and one of them already sits in both workstreams' lists word for word.

---

## The four questions, and where they belong

Product & Service Design's technical sub-group is scoped to ask four questions whose answers bind **every participant**, not only the pilot:

| The question as scoped to P&SD | What it actually is |
|---|---|
| Canonical representation of a property, referenced without remapping | Subject and identifier model |
| How the Property ID carries provenance, status and trust signals | Assertion, evidence and status model |
| How permission is granted, delegated and revoked in a live transaction | Consent model — **already in TLP's own list, word for word** |
| What partners can lawfully share in pilot; interim reliance | Liability and reliance position |

**The test.** A pilot can decide how it renders a status badge. It cannot decide, on everyone's behalf, what a property identifier is.

**What moving them means.** Trust, Legal & Policy owns the decision. Product & Service Design gets a working answer on its own sprint timetable, marked provisional, and keeps everything genuinely product-side — the experience, what it makes visible, what stops users reverting to PDFs, the pilot metrics. **Nobody waits.**

## Who else should agree

The method is worth more the wider it is agreed, and it costs nothing to extend.

Suggested: **DPMSG**, **OPDA**, **Raidiam**, **Landmark** — whether or not they participate in Phase 2 directly.

An open invitation, on the record, is stronger than a closed process. Those who join shape the requirements; those who decline have seen what they declined.
