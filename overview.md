# Building the Trust Framework by Consensus

**For Joint Sprint Zero, 13 August 2026.** Offered for the coalition to use, amend or issue as its own. Annexes: the method in full; the decision map.

A trust framework has to be believed before it can be used, and one arrived at privately reads as a vendor's product. But a blank page burns the window rediscovering solved problems, and Clarify is five Trust, Legal & Policy sessions across eleven weeks.

> **The coalition agrees the requirements. The requirements decide the architecture. Existing work enters as evidence — never as the starting assumption.**

**What Clarify produces.** *Clarify agrees the requirements and the decision programme. Develop takes the decisions. Implement writes and tests the framework.* The Develop-phase programme Clarify must release **is** the decision schedule — which gives the phase a checkable deliverable, and lets the Develop duration be set against a known quantity of work rather than a guess.

| Phase | Layer | Output |
|---|---|---|
| **Clarify** — to 22 Oct | 0 · Requirements | ~15 testable properties, ratified |
| | 1 · Root decisions | One root question per strand (~9); map published |
| **Develop** | 2 · Decisions | The choices within each strand, as Decision Records |
| **Implement** | 3 · Specification | Normative text, conformance suite, interop results |

**What the framework is for.** Layer 0 opens with one sentence, and ratifying it is the most consequential thing Clarify does:

> A trust framework for property data in England and Wales that enables any fact about a property, its title, or the parties to a transaction to be established once, and thereafter relied upon by any authorised party, in any transaction, on any platform — with its origin and integrity verifiable independently of whoever transmits it.

Stating an ambition of that size is the easy part. Holding it through eighty-seven detailed decisions taken over months is not, and that is what testable requirements are for.

**How decisions are taken.** Rough consensus, not voting. Objections recorded and named. Evidence tiered, with the tiers published before anyone knows who can satisfy them, counting only results that could have gone the other way. Four closing dispositions: resolved, provisional, referred to CFIT on a full record, or deferred against a stated evidence test. **The method recommends; CFIT decides.**

**Structure tabled, content open.** Eleven weeks will not produce fifteen falsifiable requirements and a nine-strand decision map by facilitation. Participants with production deployment experience should table first drafts of both, expressly to be attacked — a draft the coalition tears apart is faster than a flipchart. The answers are a different matter: no participant's prior work enters as a baseline, only decision by decision, as evidence, with its weaknesses stated.

**Three things for Sprint Zero to settle**

1. **Adopt the method** before the first substantive question opens, and put the purpose statement to TLP Sprint 1 as its first substantive act.

2. **Move four architectural questions from Product & Service Design to Trust, Legal & Policy** — the subject and identifier model, the assertion and status model, the consent model (already in TLP's own list, word for word), and the liability and reliance position. Their answers bind every implementer of the specification, not only the pilot. A pilot can decide how it renders a status badge; it cannot decide, on everyone's behalf, what a property identifier is. TLP owns the decision; P&SD gets a provisional answer on its own timetable, so nobody waits.

3. **Extend an open invitation** to DPMSG, OPDA and others working the same ground, whether or not they participate in Phase 2. Those who join help shape the requirements; those who decline have seen what they declined.
